After the reduced symbolic calculation, the natural next question was whether
the **full** Berton model has a clean oscillatory bifurcation. For that I used
AUTO-07p, a continuation package designed to follow equilibria and detect
special points as parameters change.

The short version is negative but useful: **AUTO did not validate a full-model
Hopf bifurcation**. Instead it helped expose why the apparent oscillation is a
hard object to interpret: the first branch was the wrong local explanation,
the canonical oscillation decays, and later formulations ran into scaling and
conditioning limits rather than clean special-point labels.

## What continuation was supposed to do

A time integration asks, “where does this one initial condition go?”
Continuation asks a different question: “if I am sitting on an equilibrium and
slowly turn a parameter knob, where does that equilibrium move, and does its
stability change?”

For a Hopf bifurcation, the tell-tale local event is a complex pair of
eigenvalues crossing from negative real part to positive real part. In plainer
terms: a damped wobble becomes an amplifying wobble.

<Details summary="AUTO labels in plain language" variant="math">

AUTO can mark several special points. The most relevant here were:

- `HB`: Hopf bifurcation, where a complex pair crosses the imaginary axis;
- `LP`: limit point or fold, where an equilibrium branch turns around;
- `BP`: branch point, where another branch intersects.

The absence of one of these labels on a computed branch is not a mathematical
proof that no such point exists anywhere. It only says that this branch, with
this formulation and numerical conditioning, did not provide accepted evidence
for it.

</Details>

## The first full-model branch was not the explanation

The first full-model AUTO screen varied `z_W0`, the height of the imposed
updraft transition. This was the paper-relevant control to try first. It did
not produce AUTO `LP`, `HB`, or `BP` labels in the 10 km to 9 km regime.
Independent eigenvalue checks also did not show a complex pair approaching the
imaginary axis.

That initially sounds informative, but the more important discovery was branch
choice. The equilibrium being followed sat above the updraft transition, so it
felt almost no change as `z_W0` moved. The calculation was valid, but it was
not probing the local mechanism behind the observed oscillatory trajectory.

## The canonical oscillation decays

The decisive follow-up was not another AUTO run. It was a long direct
integration of the canonical no-Coriolis Case-0 oscillatory setup using both
BDF and LSODA. Both solvers agreed: the altitude oscillation envelope collapsed
by about a factor of `4.8e-4` between the 150–200 h window and the 450–500 h
window.

<VegaLite spec={envelopeSpec} caption="The reported oscillatory setup keeps wobbling for a long time, but the envelope collapses strongly in both BDF and LSODA integrations. The log scale makes the late-time decay visible." />

The late-time state refines to an equilibrium with a stable complex eigenpair:
period about `10.19 h`, e-folding time about `39.34 h`, and negative real part
about `-7.06e-6 s^-1`.

<VegaLite spec={eigenSpec} caption="The late-time complex pair has nonzero imaginary part, so it wobbles, but its real part is negative, so the wobble decays." />

This explains the numerical behavior without invoking a sustained limit cycle:
the trajectory is spiralling into a stable equilibrium.

## Later AUTO work improved the diagnosis but not the verdict

The later AUTO episodes tried better seeds, log-mass variables, scaled states,
restricted models, alternate controls (`W_a0`, `H_a3`), and smoothed `z_W0`
profiles. These were not wasted runs. They made the negative result more
credible by showing where the numerical problem was fragile.

The accepted conclusion is not “AUTO proves no Hopf exists.” It is:

> the completed AUTO evidence does not support a clean full-model Hopf or a
> validated finite-amplitude periodic orbit.

That distinction matters. Failed continuation can indicate bad scaling,
ill-conditioning, or the wrong branch. It is not hidden-positive evidence. By
the end of the AUTO phase, the project needed a more transparent continuation
implementation to separate numerical formulation issues from dynamical claims.
That is the transition to the Python continuation work.
